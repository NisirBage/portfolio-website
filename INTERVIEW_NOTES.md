## Timeline thread

**Childhood interests.** Strong interests in arts and literature since childhood — voracious reading, hours spent doodling and sketching. Fascinated with retro pixel games specifically (Pokémon Emerald, Pokémon Platinum), adored the aesthetic ever since.

**Consumption → building.** "The part of the thing about consuming art in any form, is that eventually inspires its consumer to eventually start building. It was the initial love for novelty and consumption of art that eventually metamorphosed into a passion for building."

**First real build — the CNC plotter.** 10th-grade IT Exhibition project: a homemade CNC plotter machine. Team didn't win, likely because one axis didn't work as intended. First real deep dive into GitHub, documentation, and building. It stung — "weeks of effort" — but was "a reality check more than anything," and made him appreciate the technical depth of everyday devices people take for granted. Started building again afterward, beginning with web dev in 11th grade.

**The gap.** Senior secondary years were mostly spent cramming for competitive examinations, which limited time for serious projects. Serious project work resumed at BIT Mesra.

**Arrival at BIT Mesra — debate first, not tech.** Started college focused on debate and literature, not technology. Competed widely, represented the college at reputed forums, eventually became good enough to be invited by schools and colleges to chair/judge Model UNs. Pulled into debate specifically because school hadn't had a proper competition season, which he felt hampered his relationship with literature/argument; college's competition-rich ecosystem let him "experiment, learn and win in a shorter time window."

**Tech reignites, peer-driven.** Noticed peers exploring web development and started building projects alongside them. His AIML (AI/ML) degree sparked interest in ML specifically; professors pushed him to apply classroom theory to real projects.

**First real ML project.** A gene-expression clustering system using fuzzy c-means to classify likelihood of AML vs. ALL leukemia, using the Gene Expression Omnibus dataset. Started as a course project for Unsupervised Learning.

**Research — ongoing.** Summer internship under Dr. Supreeti Kamilya on detecting urea while reducing interference from uric acid and preserving creatinine sensitivity (a biosensor selectivity problem). Still in progress as of this interview — no outcome to report yet.

**Why AI became the throughline.** "AIML and data analytics is the underlying denominator across most of these projects." (Noted as a compressed answer — texture to be filled in gently during drafting, not invented.)

**Current work.** Working on the ongoing summer internship; actively looking to explore data analytics, ML-adjacent, and consulting roles next.

**Leadership.** Joint Secretary, UNESQUO (BIT Mesra's speaking and quizzing organization), 2025-26. Deputy Editor-in-Chief, Literary Society BIT Mesra, 2025-26. Now Editor-in-Chief, Literary Society BIT Mesra, 2026-27 (final year). Asked twice for one specific defining leadership moment; the honest answer given both times was that it built up through being team leader "of various competitions and course projects throughout my degree" — a pattern, not a single lightning-bolt story. Recorded as such rather than a moment invented to fit the question.

**Biggest lesson.** "The key is to be fine with experiencing disappointment and embarrassment. That's how one starts to deal with failure in a healthy manner. Another key is to always look up for any reference document or resources, and try to understand multiple perspectives alongside working on your own one." (This second half also doubles as his answer on how he learns.)

## About Me thread

**Literature.** Avid reader since childhood. Favorite book: *Johnny Got His Gun* by Dalton Trumbo — "by far the most harrowing piece of war literature I've read." What specifically stayed with him: "the idea of a man losing his agency, due to a war he had been forced to participate in, only to be practically abandoned and experimented upon [by] the very institutions he risked his life for. The entire premise about being a prisoner in your body is very reminiscent of real world illnesses like Alzheimer's, Dementia or even paralysis." Also loves subaltern literature (V.S. Naipaul, Arundhati Roy, Mahasweta Devi) and philosophy. Self-description: "If I were to do a PCA on my reading patterns, top feature would be a joy in exploring genres and perspectives" — fiction across geographies and time periods, drawn to techniques like stream of consciousness, carnivalesque literature, magical realism.

**Art.** "A huge ART nerd" across mediums, traditional and comic. Named influences: Jean-Michel Basquiat, Chris Samnee, Stuart Immonen, Ikenaga Yasunari, Masashi Kishimoto, Takeshi Obata, among others. What draws him to the mix: "the expressionist nature of their works" — Basquiat for the rebellion, Samnee and Immonen for their stylizations, Kishimoto specifically for his double-page spreads.

**Pokémon / pixel aesthetic → this portfolio.** Directly named at the start of the interview as the root of his adoration for the pixel aesthetic; when asked to connect it explicitly to this website's design, the throughline was affirmed via the broader "self-expression through tech" answer below rather than a separate isolated statement.

**French.** Wants to read Proust's *In Search of Lost Time*; was told it's best read in French. That's the entire, specific reason for learning the language.

**Baking.** Not a real interest — "not much of a baker by interest, i just help around the house." Recorded honestly as a minor, low-stakes detail, not inflated.

**What excites him.** "The practical prospects of a project" — drawn to applicability, not just theory.

**Why this portfolio looks the way it does.** "I believe that the expressionism of the self can also be explored in tech, hence this website." Expanded: "I could have gone for a normal portfolio website that is more along the corporate-esque minimalistic standards prevalent today. But I wanted to express my unique personality through my projects."

**What frustrates him.** "The sheer and awful learning curve" of tech.

**What problems he enjoys.** Problems with real surrounding context — project-building rather than isolated DSA grinding.

**What success looks like.** "Solving problems that i have a stake in, interest wise or impact wise, and perhaps making a living while doing the same."

**Who inspires him.** No single named person — "I like passionate people. It could be any trade. The passion and dedication to living a purposeful life is what moves me."
