# Content Hierarchy — The Studio

**Status:** Draft for review. Supersedes Section 6 of `PORTFOLIO_STRATEGY.md` with the granularity your brief asked for — every item ranked individually, with a stated reason, against a single test: **does this strengthen the professional brand, or just fill space?** Anything that fails is flagged for removal below, not quietly kept.

This document also operationalizes Experience Architecture's Option C: **physical/visual distance from the Desk is not arbitrary — it's this table, spatialized.** Critical content requires zero navigation from Arrival; Decorative content requires the most.

---

## Critical — zero clicks from Arrival, or one click maximum

| Item | Why Critical |
|---|---|
| Name + one-line identity | The entire 3-second Level-1 read depends on this being instantly legible. |
| Three flagship projects (headline + one-line summary) | This is the single fastest "can this person build software" signal available — the most universally-valued evidence across every persona in the Strategy doc. |
| Resume PDF download | The one action almost every visitor either wants or expects to have available; friction here costs more than anywhere else in the site (see Interaction Specification's Resume entry). |
| Contact method | No portfolio succeeds if the interested visitor can't act on their interest. |
| GitHub link | Table stakes for every technical persona; its absence would itself be a negative signal. |

## Important — one click, Level 2 depth

| Item | Why Important, not Critical |
|---|---|
| Project architecture / tradeoffs (Level 3) | Differentiates a serious technical reviewer's experience from a skim, but a visitor who never reaches it still gets the Critical-tier signal from the headline alone. |
| Leadership positions (DG, Joint Secretary, Deputy EIC) | Core to the actual differentiation story (Strategy doc Section 2), but it's a *second* pillar — a visitor who only sees Engineering still leaves with a coherent, credible impression. |
| Debate Open Finals wins | Same reasoning — strong evidence, but not the first thing every persona needs. |
| Case-competition placements + the Whiteboard framework example | Specifically load-bearing for the Consulting Recruiter persona; Important rather than Critical because it's persona-specific, not universal. |
| Current research (advisor, domain) | Critical for Professor/Research Scientist personas, Important for everyone else — the split justifies Important tier overall with prominent placement for the personas it matters most to. |
| LeetCode link | Secondary technical-credibility signal; useful, not load-bearing the way GitHub is. |

## Optional — two clicks, Level 3–4 depth

| Item | Why Optional |
|---|---|
| Full Model UN certificate gallery (24 attended, 6 judged) | The *count* is already communicated at Important tier ("24 conferences, 6 judged" as a stat); the individual certificates are evidence-of-evidence for a visitor who wants to verify, not new information for most personas. |
| Research Journal / reading list | Valuable for the Professor persona specifically, genuinely optional for everyone else. |
| "Currently exploring" / About | Serves the Collaborator/Peer persona; no hiring decision depends on it. |
| Timeline (Clock) | Interesting, not decision-relevant on its own — it's a synthesis view of information already available elsewhere at higher tiers. |

## Decorative — present, never gates information, never required reading

| Item | Why Decorative, and why it's still kept |
|---|---|
| Ambient plant, mug steam-rise, idle sway | Answers "why here" honestly as atmosphere, not content — kept because a totally static scene reads as a screenshot, not a workspace, and the Narrative System's Object Story Rules explicitly allow decorative-tier objects as long as they're honest about being decorative. |
| Opt-in ambient audio palette | Present because it adds texture for visitors who want it; Decorative because the experience is fully complete with it off (see Narrative System's Audio Philosophy). |
| Room lighting/brightness shifts across Narrative States | Reinforces engagement without ever being the thing that communicates a fact. |

## Recommended for Removal

Two items from earlier drafts don't clear the bar and should be cut rather than force-fit:

- **A standalone "Analytics" or "Data Lab" zone** (present in your example layouts). The real evidence for data-analysis skill already lives inside the Safety Platform's dashboard work. A separate zone would either duplicate that evidence (diluting both copies) or need new, thinner content invented to fill it — both violate Evidence Over Claims. Folded into the Desk/Engineering evidence instead; see Experience Architecture's World Hierarchy.
- **A separate "Timeline" object distinct from the Clock.** Two objects both meaning "chronology" is redundant under the Object Story Rules' "why here instead of somewhere else" test — there's no separate answer for why a Timeline object would exist that the Clock doesn't already cover. Merged; see Interaction Specification's Clock entry.

If, once the full certificate PDFs are catalogued (still pending — noted in the Strategy doc), a specific achievement turns out to be materially stronger than what's currently represented, it gets added at Important tier with a specific reason, not defaulted into Optional just because "it's another certificate."
