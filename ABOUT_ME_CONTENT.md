## Original (pre-Phase-H.2), kept for the record

I read the way some people breathe. My favorite book is *Johnny Got His Gun*, the most harrowing piece of war literature I've come across: a man who loses his own agency to a war he never chose, then gets left behind and experimented on by the very institutions he risked everything for. Being trapped inside a body that won't respond isn't just a wartime image to me. It's the same premise behind Alzheimer's, dementia, paralysis, real conditions that show up far from any battlefield. That's the kind of book that stays.

Beyond that, I gravitate toward subaltern literature (Naipaul, Arundhati Roy, Mahasweta Devi) and philosophy, and if I'm honest about my own reading habits, the strongest signal in the data is a joy in exploring genres and perspectives for their own sake: fiction from anywhere, from any period, told through stream of consciousness or carnivalesque excess or magical realism, whatever the technique demands.

The same appetite shows up in how I look at art. I'm unapologetically a nerd for it, across mediums: Jean-Michel Basquiat for the rebellion in his line, Chris Samnee and Stuart Immonen for how deliberately they stylize a page, Kishimoto specifically for his double-page spreads. Different artists, different reasons, one underlying pull: I want work that commits to an expressionist point of view rather than playing it safe.

That's not incidental to this website. I could have built a normal portfolio: the clean, corporate-minimalist template everyone defaults to right now. I didn't want that. If art and fiction can be a form of self-expression, I don't see why the same can't be true of a piece of software. This room is the more honest version of a portfolio, as far as I'm concerned.

A smaller, specific thing: I'm learning French because someone once told me Proust's *In Search of Lost Time* is best read in the original, not for travel, not for work, just for one book. I'm not much of a baker, if I'm being accurate about it; I just help around the house when it's needed, and I'd rather say that plainly than dress it up as a hobby it isn't.

What actually pulls me into a project is its practical prospects. I want to know a thing can go somewhere, not just prove a point on paper. What wears me down is the sheer, often unnecessary, learning curve tech demands before it lets you do anything real with it. Given the choice, I'll always take a problem with real surrounding context, an actual project, over grinding through an isolated algorithmic exercise. Success, to me, isn't a title. It's solving something I'm genuinely invested in, whether that investment is interest or impact, while still managing to make a living doing it.

If you want to know what moves me in other people, it isn't a specific résumé. It's passion, in any trade, the kind that adds up to a purposeful life. That's the thing I notice, and the thing I'd want people to notice in me.
